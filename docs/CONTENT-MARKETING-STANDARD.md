# TrendPilot Content Marketing Standard

This file is the default publishing contract for all new TrendPilot commercial articles, event guides, product guides and comparison pages. Do not ship a new article that ignores these rules unless there is an explicit reason recorded in the commit or PR.

## 1. The article must sell the experience or solve the buying decision

- Address the visitor directly in the language and market version they entered from.
- Open with a strong benefit or emotional hook. Do not begin with dry database-style facts.
- Explain why the product, event or experience matters to the visitor before presenting long logistics.
- Put the primary buying path above the fold when the exact destination is verified.
- Repeat the CTA at natural decision points: hero, after the main persuasive section, and mobile sticky CTA when appropriate.
- Never invent scarcity, countdowns, reviews, prices or stock. Urgency may only come from truthful facts such as changing availability, live prices, approaching dates or limited confirmed supply.
- Make the article useful even if the visitor does not buy immediately.

## 2. Every commercial article needs visual storytelling

- Use at least one strong hero visual and one supporting visual when good licensed media is available.
- Prefer real product/event/location photography, user-provided media, licensed press assets or reusable Creative Commons media with correct attribution.
- Do not use unlicensed images copied from search results.
- Images must have descriptive alt text, width/height, appropriate lazy loading, and visible attribution where the license requires it.
- Add `max-image-preview:large`, Open Graph image metadata and a large Twitter/X card when the page has a suitable image.
- For event pages, historical images must be clearly labelled as historical and must not imply they are the current line-up.
- Where video materially improves intent, add a short video/vertical-video asset plan rather than forcing a text-only article.

## 3. Localise the decision, not only the words

Each market/language URL should adapt the useful content for that visitor.

- Keep one source of truth for product/event identity, exact destination, date, model and verified affiliate route.
- Localise language, currency presentation, travel origin, shipping/availability context, hotel/transport advice, buying objections and CTA wording.
- Use explicit locale URLs and reciprocal `hreflang` rather than hiding all localisation behind IP-only rendering.
- Automatic detection may suggest a local version but must not trap the visitor or search crawler.
- Do not create thin translations. A market version must contain materially useful market-specific guidance.

## 4. Conversion structure for event/ticket articles

Default order:

1. Emotional/benefit-led hero.
2. Hero image or video.
3. Primary exact-event CTA.
4. Trust chips: verified route, live availability, independent guide.
5. Essential facts and honest change warning.
6. Why this event is worth the trip / what the visitor will feel or gain.
7. Exact ticket buying block with seller/marketplace disclosure.
8. Market-specific travel or local transport guidance.
9. Supporting event/stadium/location visual.
10. Hotel/stay choices where relevant.
11. Matchday/event-day timeline.
12. FAQ answering purchase objections.
13. Repeated CTA and mobile sticky CTA.

## 5. Search and discovery are only one acquisition layer

Every publishable article should produce a distribution pack. The default channel set is broader than Google and Bing:

- Google Search / Discover eligibility
- Bing / IndexNow
- Pinterest
- YouTube Shorts
- Instagram Reels
- TikTok
- Facebook
- X
- Reddit (value-first; only where community rules allow links)
- Quora (answer-first; link only when genuinely useful)
- LinkedIn when the topic fits
- Medium or another syndication surface using an excerpt and canonical link, not a competing duplicate
- RSS / feed readers / Flipboard-compatible feed discovery
- Telegram and WhatsApp share variants
- Email/newsletter when available

Publishing to a third-party platform must respect its API, community rules and account permissions. Do not spam identical text everywhere. Each channel gets a native hook and format.

## 6. Distribution tracking

- Every distribution channel uses a distinct `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` and, where affiliate systems allow it, a distinct `subid`/`sid`.
- The landing page identity remains stable across channels.
- Track at minimum: sessions, outbound affiliate clicks, outbound CTR, seller destination, locale, channel and content version.
- When enough traffic exists, compare CTA wording and content angle by conversion intent, not page views alone.

## 7. Content reuse rule

One article should create multiple assets instead of one isolated web page:

- 2–5 Pinterest image concepts/titles
- 1 vertical-video script (15–45 sec) and one longer short-form variant when useful
- 3 short social hooks
- 1 Reddit/Quora value-first answer outline
- 1 email/Telegram/WhatsApp share blurb
- 1 syndication excerpt with canonical link
- Open Graph title/description/image

## 8. Quality gate before publish

A commercial article fails the TrendPilot standard if any of these are true:

- It reads like a dry information sheet with no visitor motivation.
- It has no useful visual when suitable licensed media exists.
- The main CTA goes to a broad category when an exact verified destination is available.
- A fallback is labelled as an exact product/event link.
- It uses fake urgency, fake reviews or unsupported claims.
- The locale is only a literal translation and ignores visitor needs.
- It is published with no distribution pack.
- Tracking cannot distinguish major acquisition channels.

## 9. Improvement loop

After distribution, keep what drives qualified clicks and purchases. Improve weak pages by testing hook, visual order, CTA wording, trust explanation and market-specific sections. Do not rebuild product/event identity just to change marketing copy.
